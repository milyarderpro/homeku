## Spatial relationships in the exterior image

The *approach path* links the public foreground to entry steps/porch. An entry intended to face the path should have a compatible door and threshold. Foreground foliage creates depth but can also hide architectural identity; reference examples include both clear and initially partly obscured exteriors. Planting height, density and spacing affect facade readability.

A believable visual world retains similar vegetation and daylight across exterior and what is seen through interior windows. In [edh058](../references/edh058/analysis.yaml), flowers/greenery recur in exterior and some interior view descriptions. In [edh060](../references/edh060/analysis.yaml), lawn/trees and a parked vehicle appear in selected exterior/interior observations. These are source annotations, **not** proof of precise window bearing or survey geometry.

## Interpretive boundaries

- Yellow leaves do not certify a calendar season; snow appearance does not prove a country or altitude.
- Species of plants, geology and building materials are visual guesses unless otherwise corroborated.
- `common_regions` is architectural-style association, not a map coordinate.
- A newly designed home can borrow the principle of layered landscape depth without copying the source walkway, tree positions or garden bed outline.
