# Interior design DNA

**Scope:** interior finishing, furniture adjacency within a visible frame, lighting, focal points and visually legible relationships. A cut between rooms is not a floorplan.

## Interior coherence dimensions

- **Object orientation:** sofa faces its media/fireplace focal point; coffee table and carpet sit in the seating zone. An island faces a workable kitchen aisle, and cooktop/sink/hood relationships should be physically plausible.
- **Spatial anchors:** recurring door casing, window frame family, exposed beam style, floor material and fixed cabinetry can establish one architectural identity across different rooms.
- **Scale:** large island plus multiple circulation aisles implies a different footprint from a narrow galley kitchen. Luxury-sized furnishings do not fit a tiny footprint by merely reducing camera field of view.
- **Lighting:** separate window daylight, ceiling practicals, sconces and fireplace glow; preserve direction as a hypothesis tied to the imagined new home, not a measured direction in video source.
- **Window views:** what is visible outside can constrain which house wall a room plausibly occupies; unresolved source relationships must remain unknown.

### Caveat on continuity

[edh058 shot-02](../references/edh058/analysis.yaml) and [shot-03](../references/edh058/analysis.yaml) are separate shots of living and kitchen. Their appearance does not prove they connect through any particular door or that one sits to the right of the other. For a *new* design, that adjacency is a distinct creative decision supported by [spatial-design-principles.md](spatial-design-principles.md).

**Inference:** interior originality is visible through the configuration of furniture and architectural openings, not just changing textiles or colors.
