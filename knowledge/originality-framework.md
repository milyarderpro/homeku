# Originality framework — from reference vocabulary to a new design

This document contains **creative design principles**, not a production workflow. The goal is stylistic affinity without copying one built or depicted house.

## Seven independent transformation axes

1. **Massing:** change the number/relationship of primary volumes, roof geometry and facade outline while maintaining coherent structural logic.
2. **Scale:** adapt actual relative proportions of frontage, roof height, porch and rooms; do not call a villa 'tiny' merely by moving a camera backward.
3. **Opening scheme:** develop an original set of window positions and rhythms consistent with interior rooms, rather than transferring the source's signature arrangement.
4. **Plan topology:** create a new plausible graph of living, kitchen, sleeping, bath, hall and entry; agree on open-plan vs separated rooms.
5. **Material hierarchy:** reuse broad families or palette relationships, but assign them to different architectural surfaces and details.
6. **Site composition:** create different entry path geometry, planting clusters, trees, garden edge and views through specific windows.
7. **Visual emphasis:** identify a new hero feature and distinctive spatial character, not the same camera viewpoint around an unchanged facade.

## Example of traceable but distinct synthesis

[edh058](../references/edh058/analysis.yaml) supplies evidence for small-scale Mediterranean materials and flowered garden; [edh060](../references/edh060/analysis.yaml) supplies evidence of compact planning and some combined room views. A **new** cottage could share warm stucco-like color and modest circulation while using its own roof mass, entrance position, window scheme, layout and landscaping. That is a hypothetical creative inference, **not** an observed hybrid in the dataset.

## Risks of weak originality

- Repainting an identical facade or mirroring the reference does not create a substantively new home.
- Combining three roofs and facades without a clear primary massing identity weakens architectural coherence.
- Changing furniture decor while keeping wall/window/opening topology identical can remain visually derivative.
- Producing variations with an identical footprint, room plan and entrance but different wall colors creates superficial diversity.
- Claiming aesthetic novelty without documenting a meaningful design change hides overlap with source examples.

## Assessing novelty conceptually

Compare source and new idea on silhouette, roofline, opening positions, plan adjacency, proportions, path/garden arrangement and focal detail. No single metric provides an originality guarantee. Source traceability and visible differences should coexist; the new idea is intentionally created and must be labeled as such.

**Reminder:** the reference set may contain AI-generated or staged spaces. Source descriptions are visual references, not rights clearance, building plans or verified physical provenance.
