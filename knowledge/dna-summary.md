# HOMEKU — Design DNA synthesis v2.0

> **Knowledge only.** This is a synthesis of source-observed visual qualities and separately labeled design interpretations. It is not a production plan or a prompt. See [methodology](methodology.md) before treating observations as facts about physical buildings.

## Snapshot of the reference set

The original P1 audit (2026-11-09; source commit `80072ac`) identified **58 reference packages, 300 shots and keyframes**, and 6,226 recorded visual-detail items (not unique objects). There were 35 five-shot, 14 six-shot, six four-shot, two seven-shot, and one three-shot reference. All 58 annotated videos begin with exterior imagery; in five, exterior and living appear within the same first shot. These are **sample descriptions**, not recommended durations or universal patterns.

The median annotated video length was 20.19 seconds (range 15.300–27.233 seconds). Overall pace labels: 29 slow, 28 moderate, one slow-to-moderate. Source boundary estimates and interpretations have uncertainties. No performance metric was verified in these annotations, so patterns below cannot be called causal 'viral formulas'.

## Seven observed layers

| Layer | Observed reference vocabulary | Important exceptions / boundaries |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Exterior recognition | Recognizable facade, angled/wide approach, entry feature, nearby foreground | Some opening frames are partially occluded by trees; recognizable appearance differs by framing |
| Architecture | Storybook stone, Mediterranean rendered cottage, farmhouse, tropical contemporary, compact loft typologies | Scale ranges from tiny/small to large; a villa is not inherently a tiny house |
| Landscape | Walkways, layered flowers, lawns, woodland, tropical plants, snowy surroundings | A location or season is only visually suggested |
| Interiors | Sofa–table–fireplace/TV, cabinet–sink–range, bed–window, vanity–bath/shower | Room adjacency cannot be assumed from separate cuts |
| Camera and reveal | Dolly-like approach, lateral tracking, pan, framing shifts and object disclosure | Camera tags describe observed image change, not certified camera rigs |
| Lighting and palette | Pale render/stone, natural wood, dark fenestration, daylit rooms, warm practical lights | Distinct reference moods include overcast forest and snowy daylight |
| Visual narrative | Exterior–living–kitchen–bed–bath is common; laundry, closet, loft and exterior return also occur | Not every video has five shots or five separate rooms |

## Design interpretation, not source fact

A **recognizable silhouette** plus a distinctive entrance and landscape foreground often creates a readable initial visual identity in this curated set; whether it improves viewer retention is unknown. Repeated material pairings (pale walls/dark frames, rough stone/warm wood) can be explored as palettes without copying rooflines or fenestration. Interior character includes **relationships of objects and space**, not just a style label. Architectural form, environment, window views, and camera-facing composition interact; their coherence matters more than accumulating decorative features.

## How to read the intelligence

Use [reference catalog](reference-catalog.md) and [all 58 source design profiles](reference-profiles/README.md) to locate examples; inspect the linked YAML and relevant original images before asserting shot-level facts. Use [compatibility](design-compatibility.md) and [originality](originality-framework.md) for explicitly reasoned design synthesis, and [spatial principles](spatial-design-principles.md) for geometric plausibility. Curated, field-level claim examples and limitations are listed in [evidence-index.yaml](evidence-index.yaml); this is a selective index rather than a complete mirror of the 58 source packages.

**Exclusions:** mandatory shot timing, imagery/panel count, Flow/Omni controls, generation platform choices, cut rules, audio policy, approval gates, prompts, video QA, and file-save instructions for production.
